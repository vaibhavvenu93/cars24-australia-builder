# CARS24 Australia — Opportunity Scorecard

_Last updated: August 2026_

## Purpose

This document ranks the major problem areas identified through outside-in research.

The objective is not to select the most interesting idea.

It is to select the problem that scores highest across:

- strength of evidence
- economic impact
- strategic relevance to CARS24 Australia
- customer impact
- ability to influence
- speed to measurable outcome
- differentiation
- usefulness as a Business Builder application project
- feasibility of prototyping with public/synthetic data

Scoring:

**1 = weak / low**

**3 = moderate**

**5 = very strong / very high**

---

# Evaluation Criteria

## Evidence Strength

How strongly is the problem supported by market, competitor, customer and CARS24-specific evidence?

## Economic Impact

Could solving it materially affect:

- revenue
- gross margin
- working capital
- operating cost
- asset utilisation?

## Strategic Relevance

Does it align with CARS24 Australia's current direction, infrastructure and competitive model?

## Customer Impact

Would solving it materially improve the buyer or seller experience?

## Cross-Functional Leverage

Does the problem influence multiple teams rather than one local workflow?

## Speed to Learn

Can an experiment generate useful evidence within days or weeks?

## Differentiation

Would solving this create something structurally harder to copy than basic workflow automation?

## Builder Fit

Does working on this demonstrate the behaviours CARS24 appears to want from a Business Builder?

## Prototype Feasibility

Can an outside-in prototype demonstrate the idea honestly using synthetic data?

# Ranking

## #1 — Vehicle Lifecycle Intelligence

**43 / 45**

This ranks highest because it does not treat:

- acquisition
- refurbishment
- pricing
- inventory
- geography
- trust

as separate problems.

It treats them as decisions affecting the same economic object:

# The vehicle.

This is consistent with the structure of CARS24's business.

Every owned vehicle:

- consumes capital
- consumes facility capacity
- creates customer risk
- creates potential contribution
- changes value over time.

The central question therefore becomes:

> What is the highest-value next action for this vehicle right now?

---

# #2 — Pricing Intelligence

**41 / 45**

Pricing is one of the strongest individual opportunities.

Evidence includes:

- rising discounting in the Australian used-car market
- increasing inventory
- slower days-to-sale
- strong competitive price transparency
- Carma's reported improvements through better pricing
- CARS24's own Price Match product.

However:

pricing alone may optimise one variable while ignoring:

- acquisition cost
- refurbishment
- geography
- capacity
- warranty risk.

Therefore pricing should probably become a module inside Vehicle Lifecycle Intelligence rather than the entire product.

---

# #3 — Inventory Velocity

**40 / 45**

Inventory velocity has extremely strong economic evidence.

The structural reason is simple:

CARS24 owns inventory.

Every additional inventory day potentially creates:

- cost of capital
- depreciation
- markdown risk
- storage
- opportunity cost.

Carma's dramatic improvement in inventory days proves that execution can materially influence this metric.

However:

inventory age is probably an outcome.

Potential causes include:

- poor acquisition
- incorrect pricing
- unsuitable geography
- slow refurbishment
- weak merchandising
- limited demand.

Therefore building only an inventory-age dashboard would attack the symptom rather than the system.

---

# #4 — Vehicle Acquisition Quality

**40 / 45**

The acquisition decision determines much of the vehicle's future economics.

Potentially poor acquisition can create:

buy too high
↓
high refurbishment
↓
high required selling price
↓
slow velocity
↓
markdown
↓
poor contribution.

A strong acquisition system should therefore consider expected lifecycle economics before purchasing.

This should become the first decision point in Vehicle Lifecycle Intelligence.

---

# #5 — Refurbishment Optimisation

**40 / 45**

CARS24 has significant dedicated refurbishment infrastructure.

The key question is not simply:

> How do we repair cars faster?

It is:

> Which work is worth doing to which vehicle?

For example:

A$600 cosmetic repair

may increase:

selling price by A$200

and add:

three days to the cycle.

That could be economically irrational.

Other repairs may materially improve:

- conversion
- trust
- warranty risk
- selling price.

Therefore refurbishment should be treated as an investment decision.

---

# #6 — Geographic Inventory Allocation

**40 / 45**

CARS24's physical presence across Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane, with activity in Perth and Adelaide, creates a network problem.

A vehicle that is slow-moving in Melbourne may sell quickly in Brisbane.

The relevant decision:

Expected improvement in:

margin + velocity

minus:

transport + handling + operational cost.

This creates a high-value future module:

# Vehicle Reallocation Engine.

# The Core Insight

The highest-scoring individual opportunities are deeply connected.

Acquisition quality
↓
Refurbishment
↓
Pricing
↓
Geographic allocation
↓
Inventory velocity
↓
Customer trust
↓
Lifecycle contribution.

Building separate optimisation tools risks creating local improvements that conflict with each other.

Example:

Pricing team wants:

higher margin.

Inventory team wants:

faster sales.

Acquisition team wants:

more cars.

Refurbishment team wants:

higher quality.

Each objective can be rational individually while producing a poor overall economic result.

Therefore the business needs a common decision objective.
