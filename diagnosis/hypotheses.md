# CARS24 Australia — Hypothesis Register

This document tracks potential business constraints and opportunities identified through outside-in research.

These are **not conclusions**.

The purpose is to generate hypotheses, collect evidence, attempt to disprove them, estimate their economic importance, and decide which problems deserve deeper investigation.

## Evaluation Framework

Each hypothesis will eventually be scored across:

- Strength of evidence
- Customer impact
- Revenue impact
- Margin impact
- Working-capital impact
- Strategic importance
- Frequency
- Ability to influence
- Time to measurable result
- Confidence

---

## H001 — Vehicle Trust May Be a Conversion Constraint

### Hypothesis

For a digital used-car transaction, customers cannot inspect the vehicle as naturally as they can at a physical dealership.

CARS24 therefore has to replace physical inspection-based trust with:

- inspection evidence
- vehicle-condition transparency
- photography
- vehicle history
- warranty
- return policy
- brand credibility
- post-sale support

Any gap between the customer's expected and actual vehicle condition could affect conversion, returns, support costs and referrals.

### Evidence so far

- CARS24 publicly identifies trust as an important area of focus.
- CARS24 uses inspection, warranty and return mechanisms as part of its customer proposition.
- Some public customer discussions raise questions around vehicle condition and post-sale experience.
- Direct competitors also use warranty, inspection and return policies as major trust mechanisms.

### What would disprove this?

- Strong conversion despite trust-related concerns.
- Very low return/warranty rates.
- Customer research showing trust is not a meaningful purchase barrier.
- Competitors experiencing similar issues without conversion impact.

### Internal data required

- Listing → enquiry conversion
- Enquiry → purchase conversion
- reasons for abandonment
- return reasons
- warranty claims
- NPS by journey stage
- customer-support topics
- condition-related complaints

### Status

**INFERRED — requires validation**

## H002 — Inventory Velocity May Be a Major Economic Lever

### Hypothesis

Because CARS24 owns vehicle inventory, every additional day a vehicle remains unsold ties up capital and may create:

- financing/cost-of-capital expense
- depreciation exposure
- storage cost
- repricing pressure
- opportunity cost

Improving inventory velocity may therefore produce significant working-capital and margin benefits.

### Questions

- What is median days-to-sale?
- How does ageing vary by make/model/price/location?
- When are vehicles repriced?
- What predicts slow-moving inventory?
- What happens to margin as inventory ages?

### Status

**STRUCTURAL HYPOTHESIS**

## H003 — Seller Conversion May Constrain Inventory Growth

CARS24 needs attractive vehicle supply before it can expand buyer choice.

Potential friction may exist between:

**valuation → inspection → final offer → seller acceptance → acquisition**

We need to understand where sellers drop out and why.

Possible drivers:

- valuation expectations
- inspection friction
- competing dealer offers
- private-sale alternatives
- convenience
- payment speed
- trust
- communication

### Status

**OPEN**

## H004 — Refurbishment Throughput May Constrain Scale

Every acquired vehicle potentially moves through inspection and refurbishment before becoming sale-ready.

If refurbishment capacity grows slower than acquisition volume:

- listing delays increase
- inventory days increase
- capital remains idle
- customer choice suffers
- facility utilisation increases
- margins may deteriorate

### Status

**OPEN**

---

## H005 — Pricing Accuracy May Drive Both Acquisition and Inventory Economics

CARS24 effectively faces two pricing problems:

### Buy-side pricing

Offer enough to acquire attractive supply without destroying future margin.

### Sell-side pricing

Price competitively enough to maintain velocity while preserving contribution margin.

Errors on either side compound.

### Status

**OPEN**

## H008 — After-Sales Experience May Affect Acquisition Economics

Returns, warranty claims and poor support can create direct costs.

But the larger effect may be indirect:

**poor experience → weaker reviews → lower trust → lower conversion → higher CAC**

This creates a potential feedback loop between operations and growth.

### Status

**OPEN**

---

## H009 — Geographic Expansion May Create Capacity Trade-offs

Expanding beyond core operating hubs can increase demand and supply, but may introduce:

- transport costs
- inspection capacity constraints
- refurbishment complexity
- delivery costs
- lower inventory density
- longer turnaround times

Expansion should therefore be evaluated using unit economics rather than market size alone.

### Status

**OPEN**

---

## H010 — Cross-Functional Visibility May Be the Meta-Problem

Individual teams may optimise local KPIs while damaging system-level economics.

Example:

Acquisition increases vehicle purchases.

But if refurbishment capacity is constrained:

**more acquisitions → more inventory → longer ageing → more capital tied up**

Likewise:

Aggressive pricing may increase margin per vehicle while reducing inventory velocity.

The business may therefore benefit from an operating model that connects:

**acquisition → operations → inventory → pricing → demand → finance → after-sales**

### Status

**OPEN**
