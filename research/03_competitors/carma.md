# Carma Australia — Operating & Unit-Economics Teardown

_Last updated: August 2026_

## Why Carma Matters

Carma is the most useful direct benchmark for CARS24 Australia because it operates a structurally similar model:

- buys used vehicles
- owns inventory
- sources directly from consumers
- inspects vehicles
- reconditions in-house
- prices inventory using technology
- sells online
- provides financing
- delivers vehicles
- offers returns
- runs wholesale channels

Unlike CARS24 Australia, Carma is publicly listed and therefore exposes more detail about how the model works economically.

The goal of this research is not to assume CARS24 performs like Carma.

It is to identify which operating levers are proven to matter in this category.

---

# 1. Business Model

Carma describes itself as a:

**technology-led, full-stack digital platform for buying and selling used vehicles.**

Its operating chain is approximately:

Consumer / Fleet / Auction / Dealer Supply
↓
Vehicle valuation
↓
Acquisition
↓
Inspection
↓
Reconditioning
↓
Pricing
↓
Merchandising
↓
Online retail
↓
Finance
↓
Delivery
↓
Returns / after-sales

Vehicles not suited to retail can move through Carma Wholesale.

This means Carma attempts to capture value across several parts of the used-car transaction rather than operating purely as a marketplace.

---

# 2. The Core Economic Engine

Carma's economics can be simplified into five major levers.

## 2.1 Buying Margin

Acquire vehicles below their expected retail value.

The quality of the acquisition decision determines much of the eventual margin.

Bad buy-side pricing can later appear as:

- excessive reconditioning
- difficult pricing
- longer inventory days
- markdowns
- lower gross profit

---

## 2.2 Reconditioning

Once acquired, vehicles require inspection and preparation.

Reconditioning efficiency affects:

- cost per vehicle
- time-to-list
- vehicle quality
- return risk
- customer trust
- capital cycle

This means reconditioning is both:

**an operational constraint**

and

**a financial lever.**

---

## 2.3 Inventory Turnover

Carma explicitly treats inventory velocity as a core unit-economic lever.

Faster turnover reduces exposure to:

- depreciation
- working-capital cost
- storage
- servicing
- registration expiry
- markdowns

This is one of the clearest lessons for our CARS24 research.

---

## 2.4 Customer Acquisition

Carma acquires customers through both:

### Direct channels

Customers coming directly to Carma.

### Classified marketplaces

Customers originating through external automotive platforms.

These channels have materially different economics.

---

## 2.5 Ancillary Revenue

Vehicle gross profit is not the only source of value.

Carma also participates in:

- finance
- protection products
- trade-ins
- wholesale
- delivery-related economics

This means optimising only vehicle gross margin can understate the actual value of a transaction.

---

# 3. Inventory Days — The Most Important Public Signal

Carma has dramatically reduced its online inventory days.

Reported progression:

| Period | Approx. Online Inventory Days |
|---|---:|
| FY23 | 163 |
| FY24 | 97 |
| FY25 | 40 |
| H2 FY25 | 31 |
| Q1 FY26 | ~30 |

During H2 FY25, Carma reported approximately:

**31 inventory days**

versus around:

**52 days for broader used-car dealers.**

This is a profound improvement.

It demonstrates that inventory velocity is not simply a macro-market outcome.

Management execution can materially change it.

Carma attributes improvement to areas including:

- pricing
- merchandising
- sourcing
- operating processes
- marketing

---

# 4. Why This Matters Financially

Consider two vehicles.

Both produce:

**A$3,000 gross profit.**

Vehicle A:

20 inventory days.

Vehicle B:

80 inventory days.

Gross profit alone says:

They are equivalent.

Capital efficiency says:

They are very different businesses.

Vehicle A can potentially recycle the deployed capital four times faster.

Therefore a more useful metric may be:

### Gross Profit per Capital Day

or:

### Return on Inventory Capital

or:

### Contribution × Annual Inventory Turns

This is a particularly relevant idea for CARS24 because it also owns inventory.

---

# 5. Carma's Direct-Sourcing Strategy

Carma has increasingly shifted towards sourcing vehicles directly from consumers.

Public materials show direct sourcing increasing significantly over time.

By H1 FY26, around:

**85% of vehicle purchases**

were reportedly sourced through Sell-to-Carma.

This had previously been materially lower.

Why this matters:

Direct consumer sourcing can potentially improve:

- acquisition margin
- vehicle quality
- supply predictability
- inventory selection
- customer relationships

compared with relying primarily on auctions or intermediary channels.

---

# 6. Sell-to-Carma Is More Than Lead Generation

Carma's seller-side proposition serves several strategic purposes simultaneously.

It creates:

### Inventory supply

More vehicles available for retail.

### Better sourcing economics

Potentially lower intermediary costs.

### Data

More information about seller behaviour, condition and pricing.

### Brand relationship

Consumers may first encounter Carma as sellers and later become buyers.

### Wholesale supply

Vehicles that do not fit retail criteria can potentially be monetised through wholesale.

This creates a supply flywheel.

---

# 7. Carma Rejects Most Cars

Carma publicly states that roughly:

**90% of vehicles it reviews do not make the cut.**

This is strategically important.

It implies that growth is not measured simply by:

> number of cars acquired.

The acquisition system is selecting for inventory quality.

That suggests a useful concept for our CARS24 research:

# Acquisition Quality Score

Expected retail demand

× expected gross margin

× expected refurbishment cost

× expected inventory velocity

× warranty/return risk.

A car may be cheap to acquire and still be a bad investment.

---

# 8. Wholesale as a Pressure-Release Valve

Carma also operates a dealer-only wholesale platform.

This allows it to monetise vehicles that may not fit the desired retail profile.

Carma Wholesale currently promotes:

- direct-from-public vehicles
- inspected vehicles
- service-history information
- 60–100 vehicles of weekly supply
- digital dealer bidding

This matters strategically.

A vertical retailer has an inevitable selection problem:

Some acquired vehicles will not belong in the consumer retail channel.

Wholesale provides another exit path.

This can reduce:

- ageing
- reconditioning waste
- unsuitable inventory
- capital lockup

Potential CARS24 question:

> What percentage of acquired vehicles should be diverted away from retail earlier?

---

# 9. Direct Demand Is Structurally More Valuable

Carma reports approximately:

**50% of retail sales**

already coming from customers acquired through direct channels.

Its prospectus states direct enquiries convert at approximately:

**1.6× the rate**

of leads generated through classified marketplaces.

This creates a second flywheel:

Brand
↓
Direct traffic
↓
Higher conversion
↓
Lower effective acquisition cost
↓
Better economics
↓
More marketing investment
↓
Brand.

This is strategically important for CARS24.

A business that relies heavily on external marketplaces can become vulnerable to:

- lead costs
- bidding pressure
- platform dependency
- poor differentiation
- weaker customer ownership

---

# 10. Brand Can Become an Operating Advantage

Carma reported improving brand awareness in NSW.

Overall awareness increased approximately:

**41% → 48%**

between June 2024 and June 2025.

Familiarity increased roughly:

**21% → 25%.**

Its NRMA partnership also provides third-party trust validation.

This means brand is not merely a marketing asset.

Brand may improve:

- direct demand
- seller acquisition
- conversion
- financing confidence
- CAC
- trust

This creates a connection between brand investment and unit economics.

---

# 11. NRMA Is a Trust Infrastructure Partner

Carma is the exclusive preferred used-car dealership of NRMA.

NRMA also verifies Carma's:

- inspection
- reconditioning
- quality assurance

processes.

Carma says these processes are reviewed onsite quarterly.

This is an interesting competitive strategy.

Rather than asking consumers to trust Carma purely because Carma says its vehicles are good, it borrows credibility from an established automotive brand.

For a digital retailer, this potentially reduces one of the central friction points:

> "How do I know this vehicle is actually good?"

---

# 12. Trust Is Operationalised

Carma's trust proposition includes:

- extensive vehicle inspection
- in-house reconditioning
- detailed photography
- visible imperfections
- service-history information
- independent NRMA verification
- seven-day returns

Carma says its vehicle inspection involves multiple experts and a structured reconditioning process.

This is important because trust is not treated purely as advertising.

It is built into operations.

Potential lesson:

> Trust may be measurable through the accuracy of the vehicle representation relative to the vehicle delivered.

---

# 13. Carma's Return Policy Is Part of the Digital Model

Traditional dealerships offer:

short test drive
→ purchase decision.

Carma reframes this as:

inspection + transparent listing
→ online purchase
→ seven-day real-world evaluation.

The return window effectively replaces some of the confidence normally created through physical dealership interaction.

This has an interesting economic trade-off.

Longer return flexibility may:

increase conversion

but may also increase:

- reverse logistics
- depreciation exposure
- operational cost

The optimal return policy is therefore a conversion-versus-risk decision.

CARS24 currently offers a significantly longer return period.

That difference deserves investigation later.

# 17. This Suggests a More Sophisticated CARS24 Question

Our initial hypothesis was:

> How do we reduce inventory days?

Carma suggests the better question is:

> What creates inventory velocity?

Potential drivers include:

- acquisition selection
- buying price
- demand forecasting
- market pricing
- reconditioning speed
- listing quality
- channel mix
- merchandising
- customer acquisition
- finance
- geography

Inventory days are therefore potentially the **output of the whole operating system**, not an isolated inventory problem.

# 18. Potential Metric: Vehicle Opportunity Score

Before buying a vehicle:

Estimate:

### Demand Score
How likely is this make/model/trim/price point to sell?

### Acquisition Margin
Expected retail price minus purchase price.

### Reconditioning Risk
Expected work required.

### Time-to-List
Operational effort.

### Expected Inventory Days
Predicted sales velocity.

### Price Risk
Likelihood of market depreciation.

### Trust Risk
Condition / warranty probability.

Then:

# Vehicle Opportunity Score

A simplified concept:

Expected Contribution Margin
× Probability of Sale
÷ Expected Capital Days

This could become the basis of a future prototype.

But more evidence is required.

---

# 19. Potential Metric: Capital-Efficient Throughput

Traditional retail may optimise:

**units sold**

or:

**gross profit per unit.**

For an inventory-heavy model, a potentially superior north-star metric is:

# Capital-Efficient Throughput

Vehicles sold
× contribution margin

divided by:

inventory capital
× capital days.

This forces the system to care about both:

margin

and

velocity.

# 21. Potential CARS24 Vulnerability

This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

If Carma can:

- source increasingly directly
- turn vehicles in ~30 days
- generate ~50% of sales directly
- convert direct enquiries 1.6× better
- leverage NRMA trust
- run wholesale exits

then CARS24's competitive problem is not merely:

> "sell more cars."

It may need to outperform on the **system economics of each vehicle.**

That means:

right vehicle
× right acquisition price
× right reconditioning
× right selling price
× right customer
× right channel
× minimum capital days.

# 24. New Hypothesis Emerging

## H011 — CARS24 May Benefit From a Vehicle-Level Capital Allocation Engine

The most valuable intervention may not be a dashboard.

It may be an intelligence system that asks:

> Is this specific vehicle worth acquiring?

and later:

> What is the highest-value next action for this vehicle?

Possible lifecycle:

### Before acquisition

Buy / Reject / Adjust offer.

### After inspection

Retail / Repair / Wholesale.

### During refurbishment

Prioritise / defer.

### During inventory

Hold price / reprice / promote / transfer / wholesale.

### During customer engagement

Finance / offer / cross-sell / alternative vehicle.

This could connect decisions currently spread across multiple teams.

Status:

**HIGH-POTENTIAL HYPOTHESIS — not yet selected.**

> The winner in full-stack digital used-car retail may be the company that allocates vehicle capital most intelligently across the entire lifecycle.

**inventory management.**

# Vehicle Capital Intelligence
